The Small Business Expenses Real Estate Agents Forget to Record

AgentDeduct Journal · October 2026 · 4 min read

It's rarely the big costs that go missing. It's the small ones — the parking meter, the printed flyers, the coffee with a nervous first-time buyer — that quietly disappear between showings.

Why small expenses vanish

An agent's day is a chain of small transactions. Individually, none of them feels worth writing down. Collectively, over a year, they add up to a meaningful part of doing business. The problem isn't discipline — it's timing. By the time there's a quiet moment to log everything, the details are gone: What was that $14 charge for? Which property was that supply run for?

The usual suspects

The expenses agents most often forget share a pattern: they're small, they're routine, and they happen between the memorable parts of the day. Think parking and tolls on the way to showings, printing and signage costs, small client gifts, business meals that feel more social than strategic, subscriptions for tools you signed up for months ago, and the mileage on drives that didn't feel like "real" trips — the quick detour to drop off a lockbox key, for instance.

The thirty-second habit

The fix isn't a better memory; it's a faster capture. When an expense happens, record three things immediately: what it was, how much it was, and which property or client it relates to. Snap the receipt while you're standing there. Thirty seconds now saves thirty minutes of reconstruction later — and reconstruction is where details get invented instead of remembered.

A note on what counts

Recording an expense is not the same as claiming a deduction. Whether something is deductible, and how much of it, depends on current tax rules and your specific situation — that's a conversation for your CPA, not this article. The goal of capturing everything is simpler: give your accountant the complete picture, and let them make the calls they're trained to make.

This article is educational and does not constitute tax advice. For questions about what you can deduct, check current IRS guidance or ask your CPA.
