Are MLS Dues and Association Fees Tax Deductible?

AgentDeduct Journal · October 2026 · 5 min read

Generally, yes. MLS dues, NAR/state/local association fees, lockbox charges, and showing-service fees are ordinary and necessary costs of working as a real estate agent — which is the standard business expenses must meet under current IRS guidance. The problem usually isn't whether they qualify. It's that agents never total them up.

What "ordinary and necessary" means for agents

The IRS lets businesses deduct expenses that are ordinary (common in the trade) and necessary (helpful for the business). For a real estate agent, almost nothing clears that bar more easily than the fees you pay to stay licensed, stay listed, and get into houses. You literally cannot do the job without MLS access, association membership, and a way into lockboxes. When your CPA asks what it costs you to be an agent, this stack is the foundation of the answer.

The dues stack: four bills, one blind spot

Most agents pay into four separate buckets: NAR dues (national), state association dues, local association/MLS dues, and sometimes a separate MLS subscription on top. They're billed at different times, by different organizations, through different portals — which is exactly why one of them goes missing from the records every year. Pull up all four right now and check: are all of them in your expense tracking? For most agents reading this, the honest answer is three out of four.

Lockbox, showing services, and key fees

Beyond dues, there's the working layer: lockbox purchases or subscriptions, showing-service appointment fees, electronic key charges. These are small, frequent, and easy to dismiss — a few dollars here, a monthly charge there. But they're a direct cost of showing homes, and over a year they add up to real money. Because they're small, they also tend to scatter across cards and auto-payments. Once a year, search your statements for every showing-related charge and make sure each one is recorded.

The cousins: E&O, license, and education

Three related costs agents routinely forget: errors & omissions insurance premiums, license renewal fees, and continuing education. E&O is often auto-drafted annually and never entered anywhere. License renewals come every couple of years, just infrequently enough to miss. Continuing-ed courses get filed under "career stuff" instead of expenses. All three are generally deductible as business costs under current IRS guidance — confirm with your CPA — and all three already have documentation sitting in your email.

The annual walk-through

Once a year — January works, when the dues notices start arriving — walk through every recurring charge tied to your license and your business: dues, MLS, lockbox, showing services, E&O, brokerage fees, education. Open each statement, confirm the amount, and make sure it's in your records. Thirty minutes, once a year, and the entire dues stack is captured. The failure with these expenses is never missing paperwork; it's never putting them on the list. For the full year-end routine, see What to Include in Your CPA Handoff.

What to hand your CPA

For each fee: the amount, the date paid, who it was paid to, and what it was for. Payment confirmations and card statements cover the proof; your list covers the organization. Flag anything your brokerage reimbursed — you can only deduct what actually came out of your pocket, so keep both the charge and the reimbursement visible. A clean one-page summary of the year's dues and fees is one of the easiest things you can give your accountant, and one of the most commonly missing. Real agent questions

Are NAR dues tax deductible?

Generally yes. National Association of Realtors dues are an ordinary and necessary cost of working as a Realtor, which is the standard for business expense deductions under current IRS guidance. Keep the payment confirmation and confirm the treatment with your CPA.

What about monthly brokerage desk fees and franchise fees?

Fees you pay to your brokerage to do business — desk fees, franchise fees, transaction fees — are generally deductible as business expenses to the extent you actually paid them and weren't reimbursed. They add up fast, so make sure every one of them is in your records, and confirm with your CPA.

Are lockbox access fees deductible?

Yes, generally. Lockbox purchase or subscription fees are a direct cost of showing homes — about as ordinary and necessary as a business expense gets for an agent. Record them alongside your other showing-related costs.

Can I deduct continuing education and designation courses?

Generally yes, when the education maintains or improves skills in your current work as an agent — which license renewals and most designations do. Education that qualifies you for a substantially different line of work is treated differently, so confirm the specifics with your CPA.

What if my brokerage reimburses some of these fees?

You can only deduct what you actually paid out of pocket. If the brokerage reimburses a fee, that portion isn't your expense. Keep both sides of the paper trail — the original charge and the reimbursement — so your CPA can see the net clearly.

Do I need receipts for dues I pay online?

You need proof of payment, and for online dues the confirmation email plus the card or bank charge usually covers it. Save the confirmations in one folder rather than hunting through email at tax time — dues are the easiest expenses to document and the easiest to forget to list.

This article is educational and does not constitute tax advice. For questions about what you can deduct, check current IRS guidance or ask your CPA.
