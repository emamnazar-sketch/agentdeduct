A Simple Receipt Routine for Busy Showing Days

AgentDeduct Journal · October 2026 · 3 min read

The best receipt system is the one you'll actually use between appointments. Here's one that takes thirty seconds and never involves a shoebox.

Step 1: Photograph it immediately

The moment money changes hands — the parking garage, the print shop, the lunch counter — take a photo of the receipt before it goes anywhere. Paper fades, gets lost, and goes through the wash. A photo taken in the moment doesn't.

Step 2: Attach it to the expense

A photo in your camera roll is barely better than paper in a cup holder. Attach it to the expense record itself: the amount, the category, and what it was for. Now the proof and the context live together, and you'll never play "what was this $22 for?" again.

Step 3: Note the property or client

One extra line — which listing, which buyer, which appointment — turns a generic expense into a useful record. Six months later, "staging supplies, 123 Oak St" tells a story that "supplies, $48" never will.

Step 4: Let the paper go

Once the receipt is photographed and attached, the paper has done its job. Recycle it with confidence — the record that matters is the digital one, backed up and organized.

Make it a reflex, not a task

The routine works because it's small enough to do every time. Don't batch receipts for the weekend; weekends have a way of becoming next month. Thirty seconds now, every time, and tax season becomes a review instead of an excavation.
