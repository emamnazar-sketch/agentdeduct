What to Include in Your CPA Handoff

AgentDeduct Journal · October 2026 · 4 min read

Your CPA can only work with what you give them. A clean, complete handoff doesn't just save them time — it changes the quality of the advice you get back.

Start with categorized expenses

Group the year's spending into clear business categories: marketing and advertising, office supplies, professional development, client-related costs, vehicle expenses, and so on. Categories do two jobs — they make totals easy to review, and they surface questions your CPA will want to ask, like which client gifts crossed into personal territory.

Attach the receipts

A total without proof is a conversation; a total with the receipt attached is a record. Keep the source document with each expense — a photo is fine — so neither you nor your accountant has to take a number on faith months later.

Document mileage with purpose

Mileage records need more than a number. For each business drive, note the date, the miles, and the business purpose: which showing, which inspection, which client meeting. A mileage log that says why you drove is the difference between a claim and a guess.

Flag the mixed-use items honestly

The phone you use for both business and personal calls, the car that does school runs and showings — mark the business-use percentage as you go. Honest splits, recorded in the moment, are far more credible than estimates invented in April.

Organize by property where it applies

For agents, many costs belong to a specific listing: staging, photography, signage, open-house supplies. Keeping those tied to the property makes per-deal profitability visible — useful for your CPA, and useful for you.

What not to do yourself

Don't pre-decide what's deductible. Your job in the handoff is completeness and honesty; your CPA's job is applying the current rules to your records. Hand over everything, flag what's uncertain, and let the professional make the calls.

This article is educational and does not constitute tax advice. For questions about what you can deduct, check current IRS guidance or ask your CPA.
